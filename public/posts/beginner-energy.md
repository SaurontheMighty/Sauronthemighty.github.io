## We Could All Do With Some More Beginner Energy
![San Francisco in the Evening](/sf.jpeg)

Beginner energy is what I credit anything I have ever achieved to. If I could go back and change anything about my quarter century on Earth it would only be to have the fearlessness to match the beginner energy I already had.

You don’t need to know anything about programming to read the rest of this post but the term comes from a blog post by Zig lead developer Andrew Kelley in response to Bun - the JavaScript runtime used in Claude Code - being rewritten from Zig to Rust. He describes beginner energy as moving fast, trying a lot of different things and “learning a whole heck of a lot in the process”. You would be forgiven for thinking he means this as a compliment, reading the full post it seems to foreshadow his later comments that the way Bun used Zig was not to his liking and was an internal example of how not to write Zig. 

To avoid putting words in anyone’s mouth, let me redefine beginner energy as I see it. To me beginner energy is moving fast, trying a lot of different things and putting your work out there. The last part I think is the most important and scariest part of all. When I wrote my first lines of code at 11, it could hardly be called programming, what I was doing was just typing out word for word whatever the YouTube tutorial had on screen. Our home internet was so bad that sometimes the resolution wasn’t high enough to make out the code in the video and I’d end up with all kinds of syntax errors. But that didn’t stop me from making my own 2D adventure RPG and distributing it to my friends. I drew my own pixel art for the game and had a whole lot of fun in the process. I even ran my own flavour of phishing scam where I sent my best friend a file promising a new update of the game but which actually just flashed his name on the screen and shut down his computer.

It was also beginner energy when I released a sudoku app for iOS despite a) not knowing anything about sudoku b) not knowing anything about iOS development. At the time I was doing a software engineering internship in Java (which I also had no prior experience in by the way) and was simply bored after work. That same sudoku app recently crossed 240K installs and has more than 150,000 games of sudoku played every month. To me beginner energy is simply the curiosity and desire to try new things without the jadedness of experience.

Beginner energy’s fundamental beauty lies in ignorance, the ignorance of all the challenges that hinder more experienced people from trying things. If I had been an expert on sudoku, I would not have attempted to make a sudoku app. Just the thought of how I would build a hint system, how I’d have to make sure the puzzles had diverse solving strategies and so on would have held me back. In a more impressive example, in 1976, Marjorie Rice, a homemaker with only a high school education found new shapes of pentagons that could tile a plane, a problem mathematicians thought was complete. Sometimes, beginners achieve things that shouldn’t be possible simply because they lack the knowledge that it isn’t possible.

On a lighter note, once I flirted with this German girl who was 2 inches taller than me because I was blissfully ignorant of the concept of someone being out of my league. Thankfully I don’t think she realized that I was flirting with her and I never saw her again. Sometimes life protects one from the failures that may stem from one’s beginner energy.

Beginner energy is also a lack of ego, a willingness to start from scratch, to go back to the place of being constantly wrong that comes with trying something new.

Early this year, I was part of Y Combinator’s Winter ’26 batch and I noticed that the youngest founders consistently outperformed older ones. To be clear, I put myself in the older category, at least in terms of being to the right of the median. By outperform I’m not talking about long-term outcomes, it’s too soon to tell, but I mean more in terms of whipping up interest, trying things and just generally doing what a seed stage startup needs to do to survive. Why? Because they were too young to be held back by the jadedness that one develops with time. Why should someone use a startup’s product or service rather than an incumbent? There is no good answer to that because in its early stages it is guaranteed to be worse. While some people were busy trying to convince themselves that their product was worthy (me! so much so that I even wrote a blog post here to convince myself...), others were busy selling it anyway and you can guess which group was doing better. From my own observations the second group skewed younger, free from the knowledge of enterprise procurement or doubt about why a large company would trust their three month old startup. They just went out and did it.

It seems to me that we could all do with some more beginner energy, some more willingness to just try rather than overthink, overanalyze and worry. This brings me to the reason why I wrote this post: I find it disturbing that the lead developer of a programming language would go around pointing at a user’s codebase as an example of how not to do things. To someone who’s new that’s quite literally their worst nightmare: for people to point and laugh at their attempt. I have tried so many new things but there are also things I have been too scared to try for this very same reason, a fear of being judged and laughed at. I think communities like Hacker News are especially guilty of this, a barrage of pessimism often meets people trying new things. A less online reader might want to look up “Hacker News Dropbox”. Marjorie Rice, would hide her attempts at tiling pentagons when her husband or children came by, no doubt of fear that they would laugh at the idea of her trying to do something trained mathematicians had failed to. To whoever reads this, no matter how old or who you are, I hope you try new things and live life with “beginner energy”. If you ever feel scared that your attempt will be cringe or laughed at, know that I’m in your corner and you can ping me for some moral support. Peace.
